Measuring vertigo attacks with a phone camera

A 30-second eye video recorded during an attack is enough to measure nystagmus, identify the vertigo pattern and compare the attack with the user’s known diagnosis.

Project documents
Project

General-purpose AI can describe the eye. EyeNysta measures it.

Recording and symptoms

During an attack the user records a 30-second eye video, and recording quality is checked while filming. As soon as the video is uploaded, a list of symptoms appears, ordered by urgency. The most urgent ones (double vision, slurred speech, numbness, inability to stand) come first, followed by distinguishing symptoms such as hearing loss and headache.

Measurement

MediaPipe locates the eye region. We fine-tune a pretrained vision model on eye images we label ourselves to track the pupil more precisely, and compute nystagmus velocity in °/s. All analysis runs on the phone.

Pattern matching

The app does not diagnose. From eye findings and symptoms it determines which vertigo pattern the attack resembles, then compares it with the user’s known diagnosis and previous attacks.

Patterns recognised
  • BPPVDisplaced inner-ear crystals; triggered by head movement, lasts under a minute
  • Vestibular neuritisInflamed balance nerve; one-directional nystagmus lasting hours to days
  • Ménière’s diseaseInner-ear fluid pressure; hearing loss, tinnitus, ear fullness
  • Vestibular migraineNeurological; headache, sensitivity to light and sound
  • Central signBrainstem or cerebellum; direction-changing or vertical nystagmus, emergency alert
